### English (300 words)

> Lawrence Kuok is a software engineer with approximately 10 years of experience across North American, Mainland China, and Taiwan markets. He focuses on front-end architecture, developer experience, and digital public infrastructure. He relocated to Taipei in 2026 on a Taiwan Gold Card (Science & Technology category).
>
> His primary open-source project is **Taiwan UI** (taiwan-ui.vercel.app) — an MIT-licensed React component library for the Taiwan-localized form inputs every developer keeps reimplementing. The motivating problem: every digital service handling Taiwan users needs to validate national IDs, tax numbers, NHI card numbers, license plates, phone numbers, and addresses — yet hand-rolled implementations of these validators routinely contain edge-case bugs, lack accessibility semantics, and represent millions of NTD in duplicated work across the public-procurement ecosystem each year. Taiwan UI standardizes these inputs as MIT-licensed, zero-dependency, TypeScript-typed components. As of v0.1.0 (April 2026), the library ships 21 components, 11 pure-function validators / calculators, and 157 passing tests against published Ministry of Interior, Ministry of Finance, NCC, and Directorate-General of Highways specifications.
>
> Where Taiwan UI differs from most open-source projects is its commitment to scope honesty: the repository publicly maintains a `GOVT_READINESS.md` document explicitly listing what the library is NOT designed for (e.g. it does NOT replace authoritative government identity verification APIs, does NOT cover diplomatic license plates, has NOT undergone a third-party penetration test) — to prevent misuse in high-stakes regulated contexts.
>
> Previously, he worked as a Senior / Staff Engineer at ⟨Shanghai company⟩, leading ⟨domain⟩. He concurrently consults for ShipBob, a US e-commerce logistics platform.
>
> His long-term goal is to grow Taiwan UI to a stable v1.0 release with active g0v community contribution, positioning it as the de facto front-end infrastructure for Taiwan government digital services.
